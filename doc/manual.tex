\section{What is Streamer?}
%=============================================================================

Streamer downloads music from Qobuz in the quality you choose --- from MP3 all
the way up to 24-bit/192\,kHz Hi-Res FLAC.  It comes in two flavors:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{GUI} (graphical app) --- search, browse, and download with
        clicks.  Requires Windows~10+ with Microsoft Edge installed.
  \item \textbf{CLI} (command line) --- scriptable, fast, works in terminals.
\end{itemize}

Both read the same config file, so credentials set up in one work in the other.

\section{First-Time Setup}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Step 1 --- Open the App}

Double-click \texttt{streamer-gui.exe}. The window has three tabs at the top:
\textbf{Search}, \textbf{Downloads}, and \textbf{Settings}.  Go to
\textbf{Settings} first.

\subsection{Step 2 --- Create an Account Slot}

Click \textbf{+ Add}.  A new empty row appears in the account list on the left.
Click it to select it.

\subsection{Step 3 --- Log In}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Type your \textbf{email} in the Email field (optional, just for display).
  \item Click \textbf{Login with Qobuz}.
  \item A browser window opens inside the app showing the Qobuz login page.
  \item Log in with your Qobuz account (email/password, Google, Apple, etc.).
  \item The browser closes automatically once login succeeds.
  \item You see \emph{``Login successful.''} --- done!
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tipbox}
The country code (US, FR, etc.) is detected automatically from your account.
You do not need to type it.
\end{tipbox}

\subsection{Step 4 --- Set Your Download Folder}

Still in Settings:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Click \textbf{Browse...} next to the Download Dir field.
  \item Pick the folder where you want your music saved.
  \item Click \textbf{Save Settings}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Step 5 --- Pick a Quality}

Scroll the Quality wheel to your preferred level:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Level} & \textbf{Format} & \textbf{Typical size (per album)} \\
\midrule
mp3        & 320\,kbps MP3        & $\sim$100\,MB \\
flac       & 16-bit / 44.1\,kHz   & $\sim$300\,MB \\
flac-hi    & 24-bit / 96\,kHz     & $\sim$1\,GB \\
flac-ultra & 24-bit / 192\,kHz    & $\sim$2\,GB \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Click \textbf{Save Settings} when done.

\section{Searching for Music}
%=============================================================================

Go to the \textbf{Search} tab.

\subsection{Basic Search}

Type what you want and press \textbf{Enter}:

\begin{cmdbox}
daft punk
\end{cmdbox}

Results appear in a table.  Each row shows: Title, Artist, Label, Date,
Duration, Genre, Hi-Res, Explicit, Type, and Country.

\subsection{Filters}

You can narrow results by adding \texttt{field:value} filters.
Use quotes around multi-word values.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lXl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Filter} & \textbf{What it does} & \textbf{Example} \\
\midrule
\texttt{artist:"..."}  & Match artist name     & \texttt{artist:"Taylor Swift"} \\
\texttt{title:"..."}   & Match title           & \texttt{title:"Anti-Hero"} \\
\texttt{genre:...}     & Match genre           & \texttt{genre:jazz} \\
\texttt{label:...}     & Match record label    & \texttt{label:republic} \\
\texttt{type:...}      & Content type          & \texttt{type:album} \\
\texttt{country:...}   & Account country       & \texttt{country:US} \\
\texttt{year:...}      & Release year          & \texttt{year:2024} \\
\texttt{year:...-...}  & Year range            & \texttt{year:2020-2024} \\
\texttt{hires:true}    & Hi-Res only           & \texttt{hires:true} \\
\texttt{explicit:true} & Explicit only         & \texttt{explicit:true} \\
\texttt{explicit:false} & Clean versions only  & \texttt{explicit:false} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\subsection{Combining Filters}

Filters are combined with \textbf{AND} by default (just put them next to each
other).  You can also use \textbf{OR}, \textbf{NOT}, and parentheses:

\begin{cmdbox}
"the life of a showgirl" artist:"taylor swift" explicit:true NOT type:playlist
\end{cmdbox}

This means: search for ``the life of a showgirl'', only from Taylor Swift, only
explicit versions, and exclude playlists.

More examples:

\begin{cmdbox}
artist:"Pink Floyd" year:1970-1980 hires:true
\end{cmdbox}

\begin{cmdbox}
(artist:"Daft Punk" OR artist:"Justice") type:album
\end{cmdbox}

\begin{cmdbox}
genre:classical NOT explicit:true
\end{cmdbox}

\subsection{Understanding the Results}

Results are \textbf{grouped by artist} and sorted newest-first within each
group.  The columns tell you:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Title} --- includes the version in parentheses if available,
        e.g.\ \emph{Midnights (Deluxe)} vs.\ \emph{Midnights}.
  \item \textbf{Label} --- the record label (helps distinguish pressings).
  \item \textbf{Date} --- full release date, not just the year.
  \item \textbf{E} --- shows \textbf{E} for explicit content.
  \item \textbf{Hi-Res} --- shows \textbf{Yes} if hi-res audio is available.
  \item \textbf{Country} --- which of your accounts found this result.
\end{itemize}

\section{Downloading}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{From Search Results}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Click a row in the search results.
  \item Click \textbf{Download Selected} (or right-click and choose Download).
  \item Switch to the \textbf{Downloads} tab to watch progress.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{From a URL}

Paste a Qobuz link into the \textbf{URL bar} at the top and press
\textbf{Enter}:

\begin{cmdbox}
https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/random-access-memories/abcdef123
\end{cmdbox}

\subsection{What Gets Downloaded}

For each album, Streamer creates:

\begin{cmdbox}
Music/
  Artist Name/
    Album Title/
      01.flac
      02.flac
      ...
      cover.jpg
      album\_description.txt   (if available)
      booklet.pdf              (if available)
    artist\_bio.txt             (if available)
    artist.jpg                 (if available)
\end{cmdbox}

If you have multiple accounts with different countries, a country subfolder
is added: \texttt{Music/US/Artist/Album/...}

\subsection{Downloads Tab}

The Downloads tab shows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \textbf{list} of all queued/active/completed downloads with status
        and progress percentage.
  \item A \textbf{log area} at the bottom with detailed messages (useful if
        something goes wrong).
  \item A \textbf{Cancel Selected} button to stop a running download.
\end{itemize}

\section{Multiple Accounts}
%=============================================================================

You can add several Qobuz accounts (e.g.\ one US, one FR) to access
region-specific catalogs.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Go to \textbf{Settings} and click \textbf{+ Add} for each account.
  \item Log in to each one.
  \item When you search, results from \emph{all} accounts are merged.
        The \textbf{Country} column tells you which account found each result.
  \item Downloads automatically use the correct account.
\end{enumerate}

You can export/import your accounts with the \textbf{Export Accounts} and
\textbf{Import Accounts} buttons (saves as JSON, useful for backup).

\section{Command Line Usage}
%=============================================================================

For advanced users or scripting, \texttt{streamer.exe} can be used directly in
a terminal.

\subsection{Login}

\begin{cmdbox}
streamer.exe web-login --email you@example.com --password secret
\end{cmdbox}

\subsection{Download an Album}

\begin{cmdbox}
streamer.exe download "https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/album/..." --quality flac-hi
\end{cmdbox}

Or with a bare ID:

\begin{cmdbox}
streamer.exe download abc123def --quality flac
\end{cmdbox}

\subsection{Search}

\begin{cmdbox}
streamer.exe search "pink floyd" --type albums -n 50
\end{cmdbox}

Add \texttt{-{}-tsv} for machine-readable output (tab-separated).

\subsection{Change Settings}

\begin{cmdbox}
streamer.exe config set-dir "D:\textbackslash{}Music"
streamer.exe config set-quality flac-ultra
streamer.exe config show
\end{cmdbox}

\section{Configuration File}
%=============================================================================

Location: \texttt{\%APPDATA\%\textbackslash streamer\textbackslash config.toml}

You normally don't need to edit it by hand (the GUI and CLI manage it), but
it looks like this:

\begin{cmdbox}
{[[}accounts{]]}\\
country = "US"\\
email = "you@example.com"\\
app\_id = "..."\\
app\_secret = "..."\\
user\_id = "12345"\\
auth\_token = "..."\\[6pt]
{[}settings{]}\\
download\_dir = "C:/Users/You/Music"\\
quality = "flac"\\
concurrency = 4\\
requests\_per\_minute = 0
\end{cmdbox}

\section{Troubleshooting}
%=============================================================================

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
  \item[Login dialog opens but nothing loads]
    Make sure Microsoft Edge is installed.  The login dialog uses Edge's
    WebView2 engine.

  \item[Download fails with ``os error 123'']
    The download directory path is invalid.  Go to Settings, click Browse,
    pick a valid folder, and Save Settings.

  \item[App crashes on login]
    Check the log file at
    \texttt{\%APPDATA\%\textbackslash streamer\textbackslash streamer-gui.log}
    --- it records what happened right before the crash.

  \item[Search returns no results]
    Check that at least one account is logged in (Settings tab should show
    ``Authenticated'').

  \item[Linker error ``cannot open file streamer-gui.exe'']
    The app is still running. Close it first, then rebuild.

  \item[Garbled characters in file names]
    Make sure you built the GUI with the \texttt{/utf-8} compiler flag
    (already set in CMakeLists.txt).
\end{description}
